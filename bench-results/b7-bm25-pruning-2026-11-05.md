# B7: exhaustive vs MaxScore over tiers on BEIR (5 Nov 2026)

Raw output: [`b7-bm25-pruning-2026-11-05.json`](b7-bm25-pruning-2026-11-05.json). Machine: Apple M4, 10 cores, 16 GB RAM, macOS.

```bash
telusur index --corpus data/<set>/corpus.jsonl --out indexes/<set>
```

Setup: the BEIR test queries, k = 10, keyword mode. One warm-up pass (which also counts postings decoded and checks scores), then single-thread latency of the keyword stage over at least 1,000 queries, cycling through the set. QPS is whole searches per second over five passes of that. Default QoS, no core pinning.

**Same results:** on every query of every set, MaxScore's top-10 scores equal exhaustive's rank by rank within 1e-5 relative (`mismatched_queries` is 0).

**What the numbers say:**

- MaxScore has the better p50 and QPS on all four sets. The more docs, the more it prunes: half the postings on TREC-COVID, 37% on FiQA.
- Its p99 is worse on three sets. The slowest queries are the long ones (9 to 20 terms after analysis): every candidate doc probes many non-essential (term, tier) cursors, and the probes save little when those tiers are dense.
- NFCorpus decodes nothing less because its 3,633 docs fit in one 4,096-doc window, and the first window runs before there is a k-th score to prune with. Smaller windows (1,024 and 2,048) prune more on the two small sets but were not faster overall.
- The PRD target, at least 50% fewer postings on 3+ term queries, is met on TREC-COVID (50.0%) and not on the other three.

**Not tried yet:** block-max bounds inside tiers, and per-tier score tables (one lookup per posting instead of a division).
